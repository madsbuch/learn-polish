Polish has a formal "you" that works like a title.

- **ty** — friends, family, children, people your age in casual settings
- **Pan** (to a man), **Pani** (to a woman), **Państwo** (to a group or a couple) — everyone else: shop staff, officials, older people, new colleagues

With **Pan / Pani** the verb is in the **3rd person**, as if you said "does the gentleman…":
- *Czy Pan mówi po angielsku?* — Do you speak English? (to a man)
- *Czy Pani jest z Krakowa?* — Are you from Kraków? (to a woman)
- *Czy Państwo mają rezerwację?* — Do you have a reservation? (to a group)

Compare: *Mówisz po angielsku?* (ty) — Do you speak English?

### Switching to "ty"
Moving from Pan/Pani to *ty* is called **przejść na ty**. It is usually offered by the older or more senior person: *Mów mi Anna.* — Call me Anna. Until then, staying with Pan/Pani is the safe, respectful choice.

### Greetings that go with it
- **Dzień dobry** — hello (formal, all day); **dobry wieczór** in the evening
- **Cześć** — hi / bye (informal, with *ty*)
- **Do widzenia** — goodbye (formal)
