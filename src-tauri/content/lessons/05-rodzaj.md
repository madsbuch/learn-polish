Every Polish noun has a gender, and you can usually tell it from the last letter:

| gender | typical ending | examples |
|---|---|---|
| masculine | a consonant | dom, kot, samochód |
| feminine | **-a** | kobieta, książka, kawa |
| neuter | **-o, -e, -ę, -um** | okno, morze, imię, muzeum |

### Exceptions worth knowing
- Men ending in **-a** are masculine: *mężczyzna* (man), *kolega* (colleague).
- Some feminine nouns end in a consonant, often **-ść** or a soft consonant: *noc* (night), *miłość* (love), *rzecz* (thing).

### Adjectives agree
In the singular, the adjective takes the noun's gender:

| | masculine | feminine | neuter |
|---|---|---|---|
| new | nowy dom | nowa książka | nowe okno |
| big | duży kot | duża kawa | duże miasto |
| Polish | polski film | polska muzyka | polskie jedzenie |

After **k** and **g** the masculine ending is **-i**, not **-y**: *polski, drogi*.

*Ten, ta, to* (this) follow the same pattern: *ten dom, ta kobieta, to okno*.
