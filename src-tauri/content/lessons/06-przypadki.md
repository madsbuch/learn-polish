Polish shows a noun's **role in the sentence** by changing its ending. English does this only with pronouns (*he / him / his*); Polish does it with every noun. The seven roles are the **cases**.

| case | answers | main uses | kobieta | dom |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| mianownik (nominative) | kto? co? | the subject | kobieta | dom |
| dopełniacz (genitive) | kogo? czego? | of; after *nie* + verb; after *do, bez, od, z (from)* | kobiety | domu |
| celownik (dative) | komu? czemu? | to/for someone (indirect object) | kobiecie | domowi |
| biernik (accusative) | kogo? co? | the direct object | kobietę | dom |
| narzędnik (instrumental) | kim? czym? | with (*z*), by means of; after *być* + noun | kobietą | domem |
| miejscownik (locative) | o kim? o czym? | location after *w, na, o, przy* | kobiecie | domu |
| wołacz (vocative) | — | calling someone | kobieto! | domie! |

### The prepositions you meet first
- **w** + locative = in (static): *w domu* — at home
- **do** + genitive = to (motion): *idę do domu* — I'm going home
- **z** + instrumental = with: *z kotem* — with the cat
- **na** + locative = on/at (static): *na stole* — on the table; **na** + accusative = onto/for (motion): *na stół*

### Two everyday contrasts
- *Mam czas.* (accusative, direct object) vs *Nie mam czasu.* (genitive — a negated direct object goes into the genitive)
- *Jestem w Krakowie.* (locative, where you are) vs *Jadę do Krakowa.* (genitive, where you are going)

### How to learn it
Don't memorise all endings at once. Learn **phrases** that carry a case — *w domu, do domu, z kotem, nie mam czasu* — and the pattern grows from them. The accusative of feminine nouns (*-ę*: *kawę, wodę*) and the locative after *w* are the two you will use most.
